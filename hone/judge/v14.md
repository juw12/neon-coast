# Neon Coast v14 - fact sheet for judging

A browser game in one offline HTML file (about 0.8 MB, three.js inlined). The judges see this sheet and
screenshots taken from the running game by the test script: title screen, first view after New Game,
driving, a job in progress, the Sol Wheel at dusk, a police chase, night, pause menu. From v4 also: a cab fare, the whole map, the helicopter at four stars, a property by day. From v5 also: Sol Beach by day, painted walls in three districts, the view from the Sol Wheel ride, the perk card. From v7 also: the Rustport quay, the Meridian crowns at dusk, the beach hotel balconies, the Rustport roofs, a Paper Round in progress and the radio dial. From v8 also: the game on a phone held on its side and upright (an emulated phone in Chrome). From v9 also: the Rustport dock apron and a Shadow Run in progress. From v12 also: a stunt jump off a ramp, the barrel row going up, a road works site and the nitro burning.

## What is in the game

- One island city, 10 x 10 blocks, four districts with their own look: Meridian (downtown towers that step
  back in tiers), Sol Beach (pastel hotels, palms, sand), Rustport (industry), suburbs and parks with street
  trees. Every building has a separate ground floor of shop fronts with awnings and doors, and windows with
  frames and blinds. Day and night cycle with lit windows and shop fronts, street lamps at corners and
  mid-block, neon trim, a moon, drifting clouds and moving water.
- Pretty graphics adds a glow pass (neon, lamps, lit windows and tail lights bleed light at night) and a soft
  vignette; Fast graphics turns it off.
- Landmarks to steer by: the Sol Wheel (a turning Ferris wheel on a pier off Sol Beach; since 1.3 you can
  ride it), the Meridian Spire on the tallest tower, red-light masts on the other towers, striped
  smokestacks over Rustport, pitched roofs on the low suburb houses, and since 1.3 the Coastliner, an
  airship with NEON COAST on its side that laps the island every five minutes.
- The city by day (release 1.3): 86 painted walls, two designs per district (a sunset palm and a SOL BEACH
  postcard on the beach, colour blocks and a striped-sun skyline in Meridian, citrus and a flower in the
  suburbs, RUSTPORT stencil lettering and a cog in the docks). Parasols and towels on the sand, sailing
  boats off the shore, gulls over the beach. Deep blue sky over a warm haze, a teal sea, golden sand. Every
  wall is darker at the foot and lighter at the top instead of one flat colour; Meridian towers are aqua,
  coral, cream and lilac instead of grey, Rustport is rust, ochre and sea-green. The player stands with a
  slow breathing sway instead of frozen.
- On foot (walk, sprint, jump, punch, pistol) and seven vehicle types (Tidal, Vanta GT, Ravager, Brawler,
  Hauler, City Cab, Patrol Cruiser) with arcade handling, handbrake, damage and fire. Each type has its own
  body outline (raked windscreens, a pickup bed, a rear wing on the fast ones), hubcaps, mirrors and a grille;
  at night every car throws a pool of light on the road. Since 1.9 the handbrake with the wheel turned
  slides the car round a corner and keeps its speed (from 24 m/s, 0.9 s of handbrake at full lock turns
  the car 84 to 91 degrees and leaves it at 22 m/s; 1.8 turned 68 degrees and left 14 m/s); with the wheel
  straight it brakes as before. The body leans out of a turn, squats under power and dips under the brakes.
- Style (release 1.9): a near miss (passing a moving car at over 14 m/s with less than 1.8 m between)
  pays $25, or $50 past a police car; a slide pays $30 a second from 0.6 s on. Tricks go on a card under
  the job line; another trick within 4 seconds adds half to the multiplier, up to four times. The sum is
  paid when the bar on the card runs out, a crash loses it, and with wanted stars on every trick pays
  double. The sums are first guesses, not tuned by play.
- Speed heard and seen (release 1.9): the engine has five gears (the note climbs through each and drops
  at the change; a low octave joins only when the engine works, so an idling car does not drown the
  radio), tyres squeal in a slide, wind is heard from 50 km/h, the lens widens up to 20 degrees with
  speed (before 14), the screen edges darken from 80 km/h and the car trembles near its top speed.
- The street breaks (release 2.0): 2,197 things stand on the kerbs (474 cones, 410 bins, 232 news boxes,
  136 crates, 225 hydrants, 48 barrels and the 672 lamp posts, which a car drove straight through before)
  and 16 road works. A car knocks them flying; what the player's car knocks over pays into the style chain
  (cone $5; bin, box, crate $10; lamp post $20; hydrant $25, with a jet of water). Slower than 2.5 m/s a
  thing is only tipped over; a lamp post needs 7 m/s and its light goes out. Smashes are not tricks: the
  multiplier stays where it is. What was knocked over stands again once the player is 150 m away (a barrel
  that went up: after ten minutes of play, since 2.0.1). Nothing
  that can be knocked over stands within 2 m of a tree trunk or within 6 m of a job stop, shop or door.
- Barrels (release 2.0): rammed or shot, a red barrel goes up (BLAST, $40, a flash, a moment of slow
  motion). It reaches 7.5 m, takes up to 26 health off a car (less with distance; 40% of it for the
  player's car that set it off), throws cars and people, and sets off the barrels beside it: one shot at
  the row of four on the Rustport dock set off all four in 0.8 s. Of the 48 barrels 21 stand alone, 6 in
  pairs, 15 in threes and 6 together on the dock; one shot at those six from a car pays $1,505 (a chain
  of 7 tricks at x3.5, with a star). Since 2.0.1 a barrel that went up stays gone for ten minutes of
  play, because in 2.0 that shot paid again every time the player came back from 150 m away.
- Stunt ramps (release 2.0): twelve, on the parking strips of eleven streets and on the Rustport dock, each
  with 40 m of clear run-up and a clear landing. At 30 m/s the car flies 37 m, 3.1 m high, for 1.1 s and
  lands unhurt: STUNT JUMP, $40 plus $5 for every 1.25 m ($190). Under 8 m is a hop and pays nothing. Both
  maps mark the ramps (orange, green once jumped), the longest jump is kept, all twelve pay $2,500. A ramp
  is a wall from its tall end and its sides; on foot it is walked up from the low end.
- Nitro (release 2.0): Shift in a car, NITRO on a phone (there it is the gas as well). From 12 m/s, 0.6 s
  of throttle adds 6.4 m/s, with nitro 13.9 m/s; top speed of the test car 152 to 191 km/h. A full tank
  lasts 3.2 s. It starts half full and is filled by play: 3% a smash, 20% a trick, 35% a stunt jump. An
  orange bar under the car's condition, flames at the exhaust, a wider lens while it burns.
- Crashes that move things (release 2.0): a parked car hit square from behind at 20 m/s is shoved 44 m and
  the player keeps 14 m/s; a hit off the middle turns a car round (46 degrees for a parked car, 116 for a
  driven one, 9 for the player's own) and its driver has no power for 0.5 to 1.6 s, so a cruiser can be
  spun out. Somebody hit at 20 m/s is thrown 1.25 m into the air. At 30 m/s, 86% of new traffic is put on
  the road ahead (standing still 47%).
- Traffic that follows lanes, pedestrians that flee from shots, occupied cars can be taken. Since 1.9:
  20 cars driving and 11 parked around the player (before 15 and 9) and 34 people walking (before 26);
  a driver kept waiting sounds the horn after 1.2 s, each kind of car with its own note, and so does a
  driver passed too closely; with no stars on the player a patrol car now and then answers a call
  somewhere in town (siren on, 24 m/s, not after the player).
- Police: wanted level 1 to 5, officers shoot from 2 stars, an arrest when stopped beside a patrol car,
  escape by staying out of sight or paying for a respray. Since 1.8: at two stars patrol cars chase without
  ramming (a cruiser slows down as it reaches the player's car) and ram at top speed only from three stars;
  a crash with a patrol car adds wanted points only when the player drove into it; one crash with a patrol
  car takes at most 40% of the car's health on Normal; officers hit a moving car less often (40% as often
  from 30 m/s). Before 1.8 patrol cars rammed from two stars, every such crash added 1.3 wanted points and
  one head-on ram took about three quarters of a car. Since 1.9: at two stars officers shoot only at a
  player on foot or in a car slower than 8 m/s (from three stars at a moving car as before); losing the
  police pays by the most stars the chase reached ($150 at two, $400, $800, $1,400 at five; the full sum
  needs 10 seconds in their sight, less time pays that share, one star pays nothing); a bar under the
  stars (LOSING THEM) shows how close they are to giving up.
- Six story jobs from three contacts (delivery against the clock, two checkpoint races, a car theft, a
  getaway with 3 stars). Rewards from $250 to $3,500. Odd jobs (after the story; since 1.9 from the first story job on) come in six kinds
  taken in turn: Rush Order (timed delivery), Repo Run (take a marked car, bring it to the garage with one star on
  you), Block Dash (a checkpoint lap round a few blocks), Hot Parcel (collect, then shake two stars),
  since 1.4 Paper Round (four envelopes round one block on foot against the clock, $550; it does not count
  from a driver's seat) and, since 1.7, Shadow Run. Since 1.4 the J key asks for another kind instead of
  taking what comes. Since 1.9, once the first story job is done, J goes from the story job to an odd job
  and back, another kind each time round; before, J named the next story job and changed nothing until
  all six were done. On a phone the pause screen has the button.
- Shadow Run (release 1.7, $700 from Rico): the first job that is neither a clock nor a chase. A pink car
  pulls out and drives through the traffic; follow it for 40 seconds, 12 to 60 m behind. The top line
  shows the gap in metres, whether it is good, too close or too far, and the seconds left. Too close for
  3 seconds and they spot you in the mirror; too far for 8 seconds and they are gone; either ends the job.
  The car appears ahead of you where the streets allow, and for the first 20 seconds a gap does not count
  against you (the line says CATCH UP), so a slow start is not a lost tail.
- Told before, not after (release 1.7): the Paper Round beam is labelled ON FOOT and does not start from a
  driver's seat (the line says to leave the car first); in 1.4 it started, then failed you for driving.
  The getaway jobs give a head start with the seconds on screen (10 s after the theft in Borrowed Time,
  8 s in Repo Run) before the police know. Every "lose the police" step now says the two ways out (get
  out of their sight, or respray at Mo's) and points the route and the minimap at the respray shop.
- Cab fares (release 1.2): in any City Cab, T starts a shift. A passenger waves from the kerb; stop in the
  ring to pick up, stop in the next ring to drop off before the clock runs out. Pay is $60 plus $0.38 a
  metre, a tip for spare seconds capped at half the fare, and 10% more for each fare in a row up to 50%.
  Fares and the best run are saved.
- Property (release 1.2): four places to buy on foot with E ($2,000 to $14,000). Each pays rent by the game
  hour ($120 to $950), holds up to six hours of it, and restores health when you walk back in. Kept in the
  save.
- Police helicopter (release 1.2): from four stars a helicopter flies in, follows with a searchlight and
  keeps you in sight within 55 m, so side streets no longer hide you; at five stars its gunner fires. It
  climbs away when the stars go.
- Finding the way (release 1.2): the game works out the way along the streets to the current target, draws
  arrows on the road in your lane while you drive and a line on the minimap, and M opens a map of the
  whole island with every place, your properties and the route.
- Perks (release 1.2) tie the side activities together: 5 packages give 25% less damage, 10 packages halve
  the respray price, 10 cab fares double tips, all six story jobs raise rent by half. The pause screen lists
  them with progress bars. Since 1.3 a card under the cash shows it in play: each package, fare or job says
  how far the next perk is ("Body armour 4 / 5") and an unlock is announced with what it gives.
- Sol Wheel ride (release 1.3): walk to the foot of the pier, E buys a $25 ride. The camera goes once round
  the wheel looking back over the beach at the city; the ride restores health and marks the three nearest
  unfound packages with a ? on the minimap and the big map. F gets off early. No rides with stars on you or
  during a job.
- Police heat now reaches the other systems (release 1.3): a cab passenger will not get in while you are
  wanted, the Tune-up shop pulls its shutters down, the Sol Wheel will not sell a ticket, and walking into a
  property you own makes the police lose you at one or two stars (at three or more it refuses).
- The first job (release 1.3) is shorter and ends at a landmark: get in the red car, drive to the job beam
  (since 1.7 it stands 40 m ahead in the car's own lane, in the first view, instead of a block away),
  follow the beach road to the Sol Wheel pier. It used to end at the respray shop on the far side of town. Until that job is
  done the car you drive gets battered and smokes but cannot be wrecked, so a first minute cannot end in a
  burning car; after it, cars wreck as before.
- A skyline with outlines (release 1.4): buildings are no longer plain boxes. Every flat roof has a lip;
  Meridian towers end in slanted crowns, stepped crowns, corner fins or water tanks; Sol Beach hotels have
  balcony bands, deco fins and round penthouses; Rustport sheds have saw-tooth roofs with glass and roof
  tanks, and yards instead of bare ground; suburb houses have chimneys. Beyond the Rustport waterline there
  is now a quay with container stacks, three gantry cranes and a freighter (scenery, out of reach).
- A working dock front (release 1.7): the Rustport south shore was bare sand in front of the quay. It is
  now a dock apron you can walk and drive on: grey concrete with lane lines, a crane rail, KEEP CLEAR
  stencils and a yellow hazard edge. On the quay behind it: a painted edge, mooring bollards, crane rails,
  four floodlight masts and three yard tractors with a box on the trailer. Both maps draw the dock.
- Smoother edges (release 1.4): a second anti-aliasing pass (FXAA) on top of the 4x MSAA in Pretty graphics.
- A learner phase (release 1.4): until the first job is done, crimes bring no stars, so the first minute
  cannot end in an arrest. One line says so; after the first job a line says the police are now watching.
- A quieter screen (release 1.3): the HUD is cash and health; the stars appear only when you are wanted, the
  weapon line only for five seconds after it changes or when ammo is low, and the clock moved to the map and
  pause screens. The pause screen is three buttons, settings and perks; the map legend moved onto the map
  itself (M). The title screen shows the keys you need first (Sprint among them since 1.4) and folds the rest away.
- Every gain or loss of money shows as a green or red amount under the cash figure.
- The title screen has a "what changed" line that opens the release history (1.0 to 2.0).
- Harbor courier shift (release 1.1, from the title screen or G in free roam): three timed stops, $450 plus
  a time bonus, best time saved; checked by `test/harbor.py`.
- Money sinks: property, respray and repair $150 ($75 with the perk), ammo $80, hospital and bail fees of 10% of cash, and the Tune-up
  shop in three stages ($1,500, $3,000, $6,000; each stage adds 14% acceleration and 7% top speed to the
  car you drive, kept in the save).
- Each hidden package pays $100, all 15 pay a $5,000 bonus.
- Health pickups, a round minimap with markers, district names, a car radio with
  synth stations made in the browser (no audio files). Since 1.4 the three stations are three styles, not
  one tune at three speeds: Neon FM (synthwave, 104 bpm, driving bass), Coast 98 (beach pop, 122 bpm,
  off-beat bass, claps and a tune on top) and Low Tide (late night, 84 bpm, half-time swing, long bass,
  seventh chords, bells). The dial crackles between stations and names the style; a limiter on the master
  output keeps loud moments from clipping.
- Title screen with settings (difficulty Easy / Normal / Hard, graphics Fast / Pretty, sound), a controls
  list, pause with Resume / Restart / Main menu, progress saved in the browser. A new player who stands
  still for 3.5 seconds is shown the walking keys; one who sets off never sees them.
- Tests in the repository: `test/gates.py` (10 gates), `test/harbor.py` (the courier shift), `test/city.py`
  (55 checks since 1.9, where the J check became two for the new rule: cab fares, property, perks, helicopter, route, map, old saves, for 1.3 the safehouse rule,
  the wheel ride, the refusals while wanted and the perk card, for 1.4 the learner phase, the J key,
  the Paper Round and the building outlines, and for 1.7 the near first beam, the ON FOOT start rule,
  Shadow Run seen, lost and the slow start, the head start and the respray route), `test/radio.py` (new in 1.4: 14 checks that measure the
  three stations at the master output: loudness, no clipping, the beat repeating at each station's own
  tempo, different tone) and `test/drive.py`, which plays with key presses only (W A S D, F and Space,
  nothing moved by script). Since 1.4 it plays three story jobs instead of one: job 1 First Wheels, job 2
  Hot Delivery (against the 80 s clock) and job 4 Borrowed Time (a car theft with the police after it).
  Since 1.7 it also plays job 3 Midnight Sprint and job 6 Coastline Run (the two races), job 5 Heat Wave
  (the three-star getaway) and Shadow Run. New in 1.8: `test/chase.py` (25 checks since 2.2, 22 in 2.1, 13 before, that set up crashes with
  a patrol car, police gunfire and a pursuit and let the game run them: who gets the wanted points, the 40%
  limit of one crash, hits on a standing and on a fast car, braking at two stars and ramming at three) and
  `test/chase_bench.py` (not pass/fail: how long a car lasts with the police after it). New in 1.9:
  `test/thrill.py` (34 checks on the game's own clock: the slide, near misses and the chain, the two-star
  hold-fire, getaway pay, the patrol call, horns, lens and edge darkening, the engine's gears, odd jobs
  from job one, the crowd; the 1.8 build passes 11 of them). In `test/chase.py` the two checks of gunfire
  at a moving car now run at three stars, because two stars no longer fires at one. New in 2.0:
  `test/havoc.py` (47 checks since 2.0.1, 46 before, on the game's own clock: what stands on the street and where it may not
  stand, cones, a bin, a hydrant, a lamp post, a barrel and its blast, the chain of four on the dock,
  the ramps from both ends and on foot, the nitro tank and its button on a phone, a shoved and a spun
  car, a thrown person, traffic put ahead, a save from 1.9, a tip that waits for a free hint line; the 1.9
  build passes 2 of the first 45) and
  `.github/workflows/suites.yml`, which runs every suite on a GitHub computer for each pull request,
  for the new build and for the last release side by side. New in 2.0.1: `test/pace.py` (11 checks: the
  game keeps real time at 30 and 12 frames a second and the radio its beat at 4), `test/economy.py` and
  `test/barrel_rows.py` (not pass/fail: what a minute of smashing and what a group of barrels pay); the
  suites run on GitHub's Macs, which have a graphics card, and three of them also on a computer without
  one, to compare. `test/chase_bench.py` now keeps traffic out of the way instead of removing it (removed
  traffic made the game put patrol cars on the road, and the script counted them as the chase) and
  prints the most police cars on the map and where each crash was. `test/foot_beam.py` (not pass/fail:
  the on-foot note at every corner where the Paper Round beam can stand). Three scripts changed after a
  red run on a slower Mac, the game did not: `city.py` lets the player stand beside the car for 0.3 s
  before getting in, asks that the hint line is on screen, boards a cab from the cab's own spot and
  reads the game's own count for the eight seconds of Shadow Run; `chase.py` asks the two-star cruiser
  to come within the game's own arrest reach (7.5 m, was the script's 6 m); `pace.py` has wider bands
  for the frame rate the script itself holds the browser to.
- Phones and tablets (release 1.5): the page switches to touch by itself when a touch screen is the main
  pointer. A thumb stick appears under the left thumb (walk, steer, a full push sprints); buttons on the
  right press the same keys as the keyboard (GAS, BRAKE, HAND BRAKE, CAR / EXIT, FIRE / PUNCH, SWAP, JUMP,
  HORN, RADIO, since 2.0 NITRO; USE and CAB appear when they apply); a drag on the view turns the camera; a tap on the round
  map opens the whole island. The HUD moves to the top edge and the menus fit a 360 point wide screen, in
  landscape and upright (upright widens the lens from 62 to up to 88 degrees). On a web address the game
  can be added to the home screen, where it opens full screen and plays offline after one visit. Hints name
  the buttons instead of keys. Since 1.7 the buttons are more see-through (fill 20% instead of 42%), so
  the road shows through them. Since 1.9 HAND BRAKE with the stick pushed over slides the car and keeps
  the power on (one thumb cannot hold GAS and HAND BRAKE together), the stick is finer near its middle
  (half the stick is a third of the wheel) and on Android a crash is felt as a short vibration.
  `test/touch.py` checks all of this on an emulated phone and on WebKit; no real phone has run it yet.
- A second car (release 2.2): after a jump out of a burning car with two or more stars on, no officer
  fires for 8 s, no police car hurts the player in that time, and the hint says so ("The police hold
  their fire: take another car."). A chasing police car pulls up 6.5 m short of a player on foot at any
  number of stars (before, it drove through them at full speed). On a phone the
  NITRO button is on screen only with nitro in the tank and RADIO only with no stars on. The title
  offers the courier shift only once the first job is done.
- A chase you can read (release 2.1): one star brakes behind the player like two stars; from three stars
  the police ram one at a time (after a police crash the others hold off 10 s at three stars, 7 at four,
  4 at five) and up to three stars one such crash takes at most 25% of the car; an officer in a cruiser
  faster than 6 m/s shoots half as often without the close-range bonus; a car at zero health says
  "Your car is on fire! Get out: [F]", burns 5.5 s with the player in it (3.6 s otherwise) and can be
  left at any speed (up to 30 health for the jump, at least 2.6 s to run). On a phone the corner under
  the map shows one name at a time (district, car, radio).
- A slow screen does not slow the game (release 2.0.1): a frame that comes late is cut into steps of
  1/60 s, up to six a frame, so the game keeps real time down to 10 frames a second (2.0 ran at 0.49 to
  0.62 of it at 12 frames a second) and the radio keeps its beat.
- Graphics: Fast, Pretty or Auto (release 1.6). Auto is where a phone starts: 2 drawn pixels per point with
  the glow pass, shadows when the frame rate allows. It measures the frame rate in play and walks a ladder:
  shadows off first, then glow and half the crowd, drawn pixels last (never under 1.5 per point on a touch
  screen, never under 1 anywhere). A step down that buys no frame rate is given back; over 55 fps for six
  seconds it climbs. On a touch screen each shot turns onto a target near the crosshair, Settings can swap
  the steering stick for two arrows, and CAR lights up when a car is in reach.
- Own city, names and art. One line on the title screen says it is an original game and is not affiliated
  with the publisher or the series it is often compared to.

## Changed in this version

- Release 2.2 Second Car, a way to live through leaving a burning car: for 8 s after a jump out of a burning car with two or more stars on, no officer fires and no police car hurts the player (it shoves), and the hint says 'Run! It is about to blow. The police hold their fire: take another car.'
- A police car chasing a player on foot now pulls up 6.5 m short, at any number of stars. It used to drive through the player at full speed (1.5 health for every m/s of the hit): that, not the gunfire, is what ended six of seven jumps out of a burning car in the first build of this version.
- On a phone the NITRO button is on screen only while there is nitro in the tank and the RADIO button only with no stars on, so fewer buttons at once. The phone pictures of this sheet show what the test's player sees at the wheel (with a pistol and nitro: FIRE, NITRO and RADIO; no CAB); up to sheet v13 they were taken with every button and line switched on at once for the overlap check (that worst case still passes the check).
- The title screen offers the courier shift only after the first job is done (it starts with a clock running, and a new player could start it by mistake).
- test/chase.py has 25 checks (22 before): the three new ones cover the 8 s without gunfire, the pull-up short of a player on foot and the shove without harm. New checks too for the two phone buttons, for one name at a time under the map on a phone (seen only in a picture in 2.1) and for the title screen. chase.py: 25 of 25 on a GitHub Mac and on the slow Windows computer; the other new checks pass on a GitHub Mac.
- Heat Wave by key presses: 9 of 9 runs finished, all at the first try (the release before, the same day with the same script: 8 of 8 counted runs finished, 3 at the first try).

## Radio, measured (test/radio.py)

13 of 14 checks passed in the run that took these pictures (14 of 14 in the suites run of the same build; the one check that differs is the tone check, told under the reads that are not clean). Per station, recorded at the master output for ten seconds:

- Radio Off: loudness 0.0172 rms (engine only).
- Neon FM 88.1 (synthwave, 104 bpm): loudness 0.0444 rms, peak 0.398 (1.0 would clip), the loudness curve matches itself one bar later at 0.672 (1 is a perfect repeat) and at 0.153 for a tempo 12% off.
- Coast 98 (beach pop, 122 bpm): loudness 0.0381 rms, peak 0.318 (1.0 would clip), the loudness curve matches itself one bar later at 0.33 (1 is a perfect repeat) and at 0.11 for a tempo 12% off.
- Low Tide (late night, 84 bpm): loudness 0.0411 rms, peak 0.355 (1.0 would clip), the loudness curve matches itself one bar later at 0.693 (1 is a perfect repeat) and at -0.071 for a tempo 12% off.
- Tone: distance between average spectra (0 = same tone): Neon FM 88.1 / Coast 98 0.0045; Neon FM 88.1 / Low Tide 0.1032; Coast 98 / Low Tide 0.0918.

## Reads of this version that are not clean

- Radio, the tone check: the distance between Neon FM and Coast 98 was read six times this day on GitHub
  Macs (this build and two others with the same radio code): 0.005, 0.006 and 0.009 (under the check's
  line of 0.01, so 13 of 14) and 0.019, 0.024 and 0.032 (over it, 14 of 14). The ten-second take does not
