# Neon Coast v10 - fact sheet for judging

A browser game in one offline HTML file (about 0.8 MB, three.js inlined). The judges see this sheet and
screenshots taken from the running game by the test script: title screen, first view after New Game,
driving, a job in progress, the Sol Wheel at dusk, a police chase, night, pause menu. From v4 also: a cab fare, the whole map, the helicopter at four stars, a property by day. From v5 also: Sol Beach by day, painted walls in three districts, the view from the Sol Wheel ride, the perk card. From v7 also: the Rustport quay, the Meridian crowns at dusk, the beach hotel balconies, the Rustport roofs, a Paper Round in progress and the radio dial. From v8 also: the game on a phone held on its side and upright (an emulated phone in Chrome).

## What is in the game

- One island city, 10 x 10 blocks, four districts with their own look: Meridian (downtown towers that step
  back in tiers), Sol Beach (pastel hotels, palms, sand), Rustport (industry), suburbs and parks with street
  trees. Every building has a separate ground floor of shop fronts with awnings and doors, and windows with
  frames and blinds. Day and night cycle with lit windows and shop fronts, street lamps at corners and
  mid-block, neon trim, a moon, drifting clouds and moving water.
- Pretty graphics adds a glow pass (neon, lamps, lit windows and tail lights bleed light at night) and a soft
  vignette; Fast graphics turns it off.
- Landmarks to steer by: the Sol Wheel (a turning Ferris wheel on a pier off Sol Beach; since 1.3 you can
  ride it), the Meridian Spire on the tallest tower, red-light masts on the other towers, striped
  smokestacks over Rustport, pitched roofs on the low suburb houses, and since 1.3 the Coastliner, an
  airship with NEON COAST on its side that laps the island every five minutes.
- The city by day (release 1.3): 86 painted walls, two designs per district (a sunset palm and a SOL BEACH
  postcard on the beach, colour blocks and a striped-sun skyline in Meridian, citrus and a flower in the
  suburbs, RUSTPORT stencil lettering and a cog in the docks). Parasols and towels on the sand, sailing
  boats off the shore, gulls over the beach. Deep blue sky over a warm haze, a teal sea, golden sand. Every
  wall is darker at the foot and lighter at the top instead of one flat colour; Meridian towers are aqua,
  coral, cream and lilac instead of grey, Rustport is rust, ochre and sea-green. The player stands with a
  slow breathing sway instead of frozen.
- On foot (walk, sprint, jump, punch, pistol) and seven vehicle types (Tidal, Vanta GT, Ravager, Brawler,
  Hauler, City Cab, Patrol Cruiser) with arcade handling, handbrake, damage and fire. Each type has its own
  body outline (raked windscreens, a pickup bed, a rear wing on the fast ones), hubcaps, mirrors and a grille;
  at night every car throws a pool of light on the road.
- Traffic that follows lanes, pedestrians that flee from shots, occupied cars can be taken.
- Police: wanted level 1 to 5, patrol cars chase and ram, officers shoot from 2 stars, an arrest when stopped
  beside a patrol car, escape by staying out of sight or paying for a respray.
- Six story jobs from three contacts (delivery against the clock, two checkpoint races, a car theft, a
  getaway with 3 stars). Rewards from $250 to $3,500. After the story, odd jobs come in five kinds taken in
  turn: Rush Order (timed delivery), Repo Run (take a marked car, bring it to the garage with one star on
  you), Block Dash (a checkpoint lap round a few blocks), Hot Parcel (collect, then shake two stars) and,
  since 1.4, Paper Round (four envelopes round one block on foot against the clock, $550; it does not count
  from a driver's seat). Since 1.4 the J key asks for another kind instead of taking what comes.
- Cab fares (release 1.2): in any City Cab, T starts a shift. A passenger waves from the kerb; stop in the
  ring to pick up, stop in the next ring to drop off before the clock runs out. Pay is $60 plus $0.38 a
  metre, a tip for spare seconds capped at half the fare, and 10% more for each fare in a row up to 50%.
  Fares and the best run are saved.
- Property (release 1.2): four places to buy on foot with E ($2,000 to $14,000). Each pays rent by the game
  hour ($120 to $950), holds up to six hours of it, and restores health when you walk back in. Kept in the
  save.
- Police helicopter (release 1.2): from four stars a helicopter flies in, follows with a searchlight and
  keeps you in sight within 55 m, so side streets no longer hide you; at five stars its gunner fires. It
  climbs away when the stars go.
- Finding the way (release 1.2): the game works out the way along the streets to the current target, draws
  arrows on the road in your lane while you drive and a line on the minimap, and M opens a map of the
  whole island with every place, your properties and the route.
- Perks (release 1.2) tie the side activities together: 5 packages give 25% less damage, 10 packages halve
  the respray price, 10 cab fares double tips, all six story jobs raise rent by half. The pause screen lists
  them with progress bars. Since 1.3 a card under the cash shows it in play: each package, fare or job says
  how far the next perk is ("Body armour 4 / 5") and an unlock is announced with what it gives.
- Sol Wheel ride (release 1.3): walk to the foot of the pier, E buys a $25 ride. The camera goes once round
  the wheel looking back over the beach at the city; the ride restores health and marks the three nearest
  unfound packages with a ? on the minimap and the big map. F gets off early. No rides with stars on you or
  during a job.
- Police heat now reaches the other systems (release 1.3): a cab passenger will not get in while you are
  wanted, the Tune-up shop pulls its shutters down, the Sol Wheel will not sell a ticket, and walking into a
  property you own makes the police lose you at one or two stars (at three or more it refuses).
- The first job (release 1.3) is shorter and ends at a landmark: get in the red car, drive to the job beam,
  follow the beach road to the Sol Wheel pier. It used to end at the respray shop on the far side of town. Until that job is
  done the car you drive gets battered and smokes but cannot be wrecked, so a first minute cannot end in a
  burning car; after it, cars wreck as before.
- A skyline with outlines (release 1.4): buildings are no longer plain boxes. Every flat roof has a lip;
  Meridian towers end in slanted crowns, stepped crowns, corner fins or water tanks; Sol Beach hotels have
  balcony bands, deco fins and round penthouses; Rustport sheds have saw-tooth roofs with glass and roof
  tanks, and yards instead of bare ground; suburb houses have chimneys. Beyond the Rustport waterline there
  is now a quay with container stacks, three gantry cranes and a freighter (scenery, out of reach).
- Smoother edges (release 1.4): a second anti-aliasing pass (FXAA) on top of the 4x MSAA in Pretty graphics.
- A learner phase (release 1.4): until the first job is done, crimes bring no stars, so the first minute
  cannot end in an arrest. One line says so; after the first job a line says the police are now watching.
- A quieter screen (release 1.3): the HUD is cash and health; the stars appear only when you are wanted, the
  weapon line only for five seconds after it changes or when ammo is low, and the clock moved to the map and
  pause screens. The pause screen is three buttons, settings and perks; the map legend moved onto the map
  itself (M). The title screen shows the keys you need first (Sprint among them since 1.4) and folds the rest away.
- Every gain or loss of money shows as a green or red amount under the cash figure.
- The title screen has a "what changed" line that opens the release history (1.0 to 1.4).
- Harbor courier shift (release 1.1, from the title screen or G in free roam): three timed stops, $450 plus
  a time bonus, best time saved; checked by `test/harbor.py`.
- Money sinks: property, respray and repair $150 ($75 with the perk), ammo $80, hospital and bail fees of 10% of cash, and the Tune-up
  shop in three stages ($1,500, $3,000, $6,000; each stage adds 14% acceleration and 7% top speed to the
  car you drive, kept in the save).
- Each hidden package pays $100, all 15 pay a $5,000 bonus.
- Health pickups, a round minimap with markers, district names, a car radio with
  synth stations made in the browser (no audio files). Since 1.4 the three stations are three styles, not
  one tune at three speeds: Neon FM (synthwave, 104 bpm, driving bass), Coast 98 (beach pop, 122 bpm,
  off-beat bass, claps and a tune on top) and Low Tide (late night, 84 bpm, half-time swing, long bass,
  seventh chords, bells). The dial crackles between stations and names the style; a limiter on the master
  output keeps loud moments from clipping.
- Title screen with settings (difficulty Easy / Normal / Hard, graphics Fast / Pretty, sound), a controls
  list, pause with Resume / Restart / Main menu, progress saved in the browser. A new player who stands
  still for 3.5 seconds is shown the walking keys; one who sets off never sees them.
- Tests in the repository: `test/gates.py` (10 gates), `test/harbor.py` (the courier shift), `test/city.py`
  (39 checks: cab fares, property, perks, helicopter, route, map, old saves, for 1.3 the safehouse rule,
  the wheel ride, the refusals while wanted and the perk card, and for 1.4 the learner phase, the J key,
  the Paper Round and the building outlines), `test/radio.py` (new in 1.4: 14 checks that measure the
  three stations at the master output: loudness, no clipping, the beat repeating at each station's own
  tempo, different tone) and `test/drive.py`, which plays with key presses only (W A S D, F and Space,
  nothing moved by script). Since 1.4 it plays three story jobs instead of one: job 1 First Wheels, job 2
  Hot Delivery (against the 80 s clock) and job 4 Borrowed Time (a car theft with the police after it).
- Phones and tablets (release 1.5): the page switches to touch by itself when a touch screen is the main
  pointer. A thumb stick appears under the left thumb (walk, steer, a full push sprints); buttons on the
  right press the same keys as the keyboard (GAS, BRAKE, HAND BRAKE, CAR / EXIT, FIRE / PUNCH, SWAP, JUMP,
  HORN, RADIO; USE and CAB appear when they apply); a drag on the view turns the camera; a tap on the round
  map opens the whole island. The HUD moves to the top edge and the menus fit a 360 point wide screen, in
  landscape and upright (upright widens the lens from 62 to up to 88 degrees). On a web address the game
  can be added to the home screen, where it opens full screen and plays offline after one visit. Hints name
  the buttons instead of keys. `test/touch.py` checks all of this on an emulated phone and on WebKit; no
  real phone has run it yet.
- Graphics: Fast, Pretty or Auto (release 1.6). Auto is where a phone starts: 2 drawn pixels per point with
  the glow pass, shadows when the frame rate allows. It measures the frame rate in play and walks a ladder:
  shadows off first, then glow and half the crowd, drawn pixels last (never under 1.5 per point on a touch
  screen, never under 1 anywhere). A step down that buys no frame rate is given back; over 55 fps for six
  seconds it climbs. On a touch screen each shot turns onto a target near the crosshair, Settings can swap
  the steering stick for two arrows, and CAR lights up when a car is in reach.
- Own city, names and art. One line on the title screen says it is an original game and is not affiliated
  with the publisher or the series it is often compared to.

## Changed in this version

- Release 1.6 'Sharp'. Graphics has a third choice, Auto, and a phone starts on it: 2 drawn pixels per point with the neon glow (1.5 started phones on Fast: 1.5 pixels per point, no glow, no shadows), shadows added when the frame rate allows.
- Auto measures while you play: below 40 frames a second it gives up shadows first, then the glow and half the crowd, and drawn pixels last, never under 1.5 per point on a touch screen; above 55 for a while it climbs again.
- On a touch screen each shot first turns the view onto the nearest target close to the crosshair (police first when wanted).
- Settings, Steering: Stick or Arrows. Arrows puts two buttons under the left thumb while driving.
- CAR lights up when a car is close enough to take; buttons tick under the thumb on Android.

## Radio, measured (test/radio.py)

14 of 14 checks passed. Per station, recorded at the master output for ten seconds:

- Radio Off: loudness 0.0189 rms (engine only).
- Neon FM 88.1 (synthwave, 104 bpm): loudness 0.0463 rms, peak 0.403 (1.0 would clip), the loudness curve matches itself one bar later at 0.46 (1 is a perfect repeat) and at 0.062 for a tempo 12% off.
- Coast 98 (beach pop, 122 bpm): loudness 0.0421 rms, peak 0.312 (1.0 would clip), the loudness curve matches itself one bar later at 0.419 (1 is a perfect repeat) and at 0.109 for a tempo 12% off.
- Low Tide (late night, 84 bpm): loudness 0.0402 rms, peak 0.342 (1.0 would clip), the loudness curve matches itself one bar later at 0.536 (1 is a perfect repeat) and at -0.076 for a tempo 12% off.
- Tone: distance between average spectra (0 = same tone): Neon FM 88.1 / Coast 98 0.034; Neon FM 88.1 / Low Tide 0.0637; Coast 98 / Low Tide 0.1144.

The radio code is the same in releases 1.4 to 1.7. The numbers above were recorded on 1.7 in a quieter hour.
On the busiest hour of the same evening release 1.6 read 11, 9, 12 and 10 of 14 (timing and loudness checks,
which a stuttering browser fails), and 1.5-based builds read 12 of 14 once.

## Not covered by the tests

- How the driving feels to a person. The radio is measured (in time, three tempos, different tone, no
  clipping) but nobody has listened to it: whether the music is pleasant is not known.
- Real input: test/drive.py plays jobs with key presses only (W A S D, F, Space and J, nothing moved by
  script; for every job but the first the save file is first set to 'earlier jobs done'). A script is not a
  person: it reads the game state and never gets lost, and it cannot say whether a job is fun. Results (measured on release 1.4; 1.5 and 1.6 did not change the driving or the jobs; nothing was played on 1.6 itself):
  - Job 1 (First Wheels): finished 5 of 5 runs, in 38.5 to 50.9 s.
  - Job 2 (Hot Delivery): finished 3 of 3 runs, in 110.2 to 132.0 s.
  - Job 4 (Borrowed Time): finished 2 of 3 runs, in 169.1 to 317.4 s, tries per run [1, 2, 2], arrested or taken out 4 times on the way; the failed runs ended as: driving to the job beam.
  - The same script on release 1.5 (same driving code) on a later, busy day (the PC was running other work, two or three
    browser runs at a time): job 4 finished 0 of 2 runs (the stolen car was wrecked by police cars 9 to 13 s
    after the two stars came on); job 2 finished 0 of 1 (300 s, taken out once, up to four stars), while
    release 1.4 in the same hour finished job 2 in 146.4 s.
- Passed by script only (the car or the player is moved between markers), so their difficulty and their
  time limits are not measured: the two races (jobs 3 and 6), the three-star getaway (job 5), the odd jobs, the cab fares and the courier shift.
- Tested in Chrome on one Windows laptop and (releases 1.5 and 1.6) on one Mac. Since 1.5 there are touch controls for
  phones and tablets; test/touch.py checks them on an emulated phone in Chrome (ten checks; 10 of 10 on the Mac; 7 of 10 on this busy laptop: two phone sizes timed out and the Safari-engine check drew only 3 to 9 frames in 1.5 s; that check alone failed 2 of 2 more runs on this laptop).
  No real phone or tablet has run the game yet, so thumb reach, heat and frame rate on a phone are not known.
  No gamepad controls.
- No real players have tried it yet. The scores are from AI judges reading this sheet and the screenshots.
