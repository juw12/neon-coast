NEON COAST

An original open-city sandbox in one file. Take a car, run jobs, drive a cab, buy property, lose the police.

Works upright, but turn the phone on its side for a wider view of the road.

Version 2.0 · Havoc — the street breaks: cones, bins, hydrants, barrels and lamp posts fly and pay, twelve stunt ramps, a nitro tank that tricks fill, and a hard hit spins a car round. What changed, release by release
  • 2.1 · Way Out — A chase you can read. At one star a cruiser now slows down behind you, as it does at two stars; it used to ram at full speed. From three stars the police still ram, but one at a time: after a police car has hit you the others hold off for 10 seconds at three stars, 7 at four and 4 at five, and up to three stars one such crash takes at most 25% of your car on Normal, not 40%. An officer in a cruiser on the move shoots half as often and gets nothing for being close; one who has stopped shoots as before. A car at zero health now says so: “Your car is on fire! Get out”, and with you in it the fuse is 5.5 seconds, not 3.6. You can leave a burning car at any speed: a jump from a fast one costs up to 30 health, and you then have at least 2.6 seconds to run. On a phone the corner under the map shows one name at a time: the district, then the car, then the radio station.
  • 2.0.1 — On a slow computer or phone the game no longer runs in slow motion. A slow frame is now cut into steps of a sixtieth of a second, so the game keeps real time down to 10 frames a second; at 12 frames a second 2.0 ran at half to six tenths of its speed. The radio keeps its beat when a frame comes late. One rule changed: a barrel that went up now stands again after ten minutes of play, not as soon as you are 150 m away. One shot at the six barrels on the Rustport dock paid $1,505 every time you came back. Nothing else in the game changed.
  • 2.0 · Havoc — The street breaks. Cones, bins, news boxes, crates, hydrants, barrels and lamp posts no longer stand like walls or like air: a car knocks them flying. What your car knocks over pays into the chain ($5 to $25), a hydrant throws water, a lamp post comes down and its light goes out, and a red barrel goes up, throws the cars round it and sets off the barrels next to it; a shot sets one off too. Twelve stunt ramps stand on the parking strips and on the Rustport dock: the further the jump, the more it pays, the big map marks them, and all twelve pay $2,500. Tricks, smashes and jumps fill a nitro tank: Shift, or the NITRO button, burns it for a harder pull and a higher top speed. A hard hit off the middle turns a car round and its driver loses it for a moment, so a cruiser can be spun out; a parked car is shoved down the street. Somebody hit at speed is thrown into the air. At speed most new traffic appears on the road ahead, and nothing parks on a ramp.
  • 1.9 · Sideways — Hold the handbrake with the wheel turned and the car slides round the corner and keeps most of its speed; with the wheel straight it is the brake it was. Passing another car closely at speed is a near miss worth $25 ($50 past a police car), and a slide pays $30 a second. Tricks in a row multiply, up to four times; the sum is paid when the bar on the card runs out, and a crash loses it. With wanted stars on, every trick pays double. Losing the police on the road pays $150 at two stars, $400, $800 and $1,400 above, in full when they had you in sight for ten seconds; a bar under the stars shows how close you are to losing them. At two stars officers no longer shoot at a moving car. Odd jobs open after the first story job: J goes from the story job to an odd job and back. More cars and people on the streets, drivers sound their horns, and now and then a patrol car runs past on a call. The engine has gears, tyres squeal, wind is heard at speed, the car body leans, and the view widens and darkens at the edges when you drive fast. On a phone a slide keeps the power on by itself and the stick is finer near its middle.
  • 1.8 · Fair Cop — At two stars the police chase you without ramming: a cruiser slows down as it reaches your car and stays on your bumper. From three stars they ram, as before. A crash with a police car adds to your stars only when you drove into it: when the cruiser rams you, it does not. One crash with a cruiser, however hard, takes at most 40% of your car's health on Normal, also when it shoves you into a wall. Officers hit a moving car less often: the faster you drive, the worse they shoot, and from 30 m/s (108 km/h) they hit less than half as often as they hit a standing car. Driving into the police yourself counts as before, and so does every other crash.
  • 1.7 · Tail Lights — Shadow Run, a sixth odd job with no clock and no marker: stay 12 to 60 m behind a pink car for 40 seconds. The first job beam stands in the red car's own lane, 40 m ahead. Paper Round says ON FOOT on its beam and waits until you leave the car. Ten seconds of head start when you take the marked car in Borrowed Time. While you lose the police in a job, the road arrows lead to Mo's Respray. The shore in front of the Rustport quay is a dock apron with a lane, a crane rail and bollards. On a phone the buttons are more see-through, so the road shows behind them.
  • 1.6 · Sharp — Graphics has a third choice, Auto, and a phone starts on it: twice the drawn pixels of 1.5, the neon glow, and shadows when the frame rate allows. It measures as you play, gives up shadows first and sharpness last, and takes back what it gave up when the phone is smooth again. On a touch screen each shot turns onto a target near the crosshair, the CAR button lights up when a car is in reach, and Settings can swap the steering stick for two arrows.
  • 1.5 · Pocket — Touch controls for phones and tablets: a thumb stick to walk and steer, GAS and BRAKE, drag the view to look, tap the round map for the whole island. The screen rearranges itself for landscape and for upright. On a web address it can be added to the home screen, where it opens full screen and plays offline. Keyboard and mouse play as before.
  • 1.4 · Skyline — Roof edges, crowns and fins on the Meridian towers, balconies on the Sol Beach hotels, saw-tooth factory roofs and tanks in Rustport, and a quay with cranes, containers and a freighter off the docks. Until your first job is done the police let you off. After the story, J picks the kind of odd job, and a new one is done on foot. The three radio stations now differ in drums, bass and tune, not only in speed. Smoother edges on Pretty graphics.
  • 1.3 · Clear Day — The first job is a short run down the beach road to the Sol Wheel. Ride the wheel for $25: it rests you and marks hidden packages on the map. Stars, pistol and clock leave the screen when they do not matter; perks report their progress as you play. Police heat now shuts the tune-up shop and keeps cab fares out of the car, and a property you own hides you from two stars. By day: murals, an airship, gulls, a warmer sky and sea.
  • 1.2 · City Lights — Cab fares in any City Cab (T). Four properties to buy that pay rent. A police helicopter at four stars. Arrows on the road and a line on the map to every target, and the whole map on M. Four perks earned by packages, fares and story jobs. Four kinds of odd job after the story. New look: shop fronts, stepped towers, street trees, detailed cars, moving water, clouds, and glow at night. Existing saves carry over.
  • 1.1 · Harbor shift — Three courier stops, a ferry deadline and a saved personal best. Landmarks to steer by, the Tune-up shop.
  • 1.0 — The island, six story jobs, police with five wanted levels, fifteen hidden packages.
